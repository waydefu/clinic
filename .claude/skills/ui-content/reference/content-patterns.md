# Organise actual source-backed content

Use this reference on the actual authorised content. The owner rejected
self-manufactured teaching samples; none are retained here as copy or design
authority. Reading structure should support the international-level visual
brief in [award benchmarks](../../ui-design/reference/award-benchmarks.md),
while medical meaning and review stay governed by the source procedure.

## Preserve meaning while making language familiar

For clinical rewriting, first extract the subject, assertion, conditions,
numbers/units and uncertainty from the identified source. Shorten sentence
construction and replace unfamiliar terms only with a sourced explanation.
Compare those fields again after editing. Retain a source's uncertainty and
eligible group; do not turn a relative comparison into a guarantee or supply
a percentage/recovery time to make the prose appear concrete.

Put the main message where it answers the visitor's question. A plain-language
rewrite can be concise without deleting qualifications or changing who does
what. Read the revised copy aloud and inspect its Chinese wrapping in the real
composition. A neat sentence still needs medical review when it makes a claim.

## Content shape follows the reading task

Orient a symptom article from the visitor's familiar situation to its existing
scope and sourced explanations. Introduce anatomy before presenting the intact
figure and readable equivalent. Establish a comparison's criteria/basis before
the table, followed by its qualifying notes. Use ordered treatment only for a
real sequence already in the source. A physician profile groups name, specialty,
care role and credentials without implying individual booking.

These relationships are choices, not a mandatory article template. Keep the
first useful explanation visible. Secondary detail can follow, but necessary
conditions must not disappear inside an unrelated collapsed block. Distinct
content can have distinct reading rhythm within one brand language.

## Comparisons retain their axes

Build the axes before markup. Use like-for-like criteria, consistent units,
clear entries and associated source notes. Resolve a source symbol's meaning
before converting it to words; a decorative crown/cross/circle does not authorise
a ranking, universal suitability or an effectiveness promise.

Use a native table with caption and appropriate row/column headers for tabular
relationships. On mobile, preserve the comparison axis in a labelled scroll
region and provide its sourced first-level explanation before the full table.
Do not replace nine criteria with unrelated cards or omit columns to fit.
Missing-source information and “not applicable” remain different states.

An intact image can accompany the readable comparison. Check the transcription
cell-by-cell, including conditions; record unreadable/conflicting labels for
clinical review instead of extrapolating from adjacent entries.

## Complete figures need readable equivalence

Identify the figure briefly, then expose its meaningful labels, categories,
sequence and relationships as normal structured HTML. Keep the whole source
canvas and aspect ratio. A short alt or enlargement alone does not convey a
complex figure; a long `aria-describedby` paragraph alone also loses heading
and table navigation for screen-reader use. Associate a nearby structured
explanation without hiding its first-level knowledge behind an operation.

Inspect the original image and its explanation together. Preserve source order,
terms and meaning; retain ambiguities as pending review. Do not crop protected
paid artwork into new icons or guess anatomy to fill a visual composition.

## Actions name their destination

Name the topic/action offered, check the real destination and preserve native
semantics. Different entries must not silently lead to an unrelated common
placeholder. Keep visible/accessible naming coherent and the booking boundary
separate. Do not import a reference site's commerce, testimonials or
physician-specific booking just because its visual work won an award.

## Review evidence and provenance

Judge whether the reader can find the subject, understand the first useful
explanation and identify the next action, then compare clinical meanings and
inspect the actual desktop/mobile page. Automated readability scores are
diagnostic prompts, not clinical approval or a mandatory passing score.

The [CDC Clear Communication Index](https://www.cdc.gov/ccindex/index.html)
informs audience, main message, action and understandable language, not a new
Chinese readability gate. W3C guidance on
[tables](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/tables/) and
[complex images](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/complex/) informs native
relationships and structured descriptions. These sources do not approve medical
claims or define the project's visual excellence. Reviewed 2026-10-08.
